# The Great Unbundling: How Agentic Commerce Will Reshape Every Go-To-Market Model

## Not All Business Models Are Created Equal in the Age of AI Shopping Agents

When Walmart partnered with OpenAI in October 2025 to bring shopping directly into ChatGPT, it wasn't just another tech partnership—it was a declaration that the rules of retail have fundamentally changed. For the first time, consumers could discover, compare, and purchase products without ever visiting a website, opening an app, or clicking through a traditional shopping interface.

But here's what most people missed: **this shift doesn't affect all businesses equally**.

If you're a direct-to-consumer brand like Warby Parker, this is your moment. If you're a product stuck on Amazon's third-party marketplace, this might be your death knell. And if you're a traditional department store without your own brands, you're staring at an existential crisis.

The rise of agentic commerce—where AI agents autonomously shop on behalf of consumers—is about to radically restructure the entire retail landscape. But the impact depends entirely on *how* you go to market.

Let me show you exactly how this plays out for each business model, who wins, who loses, and what you need to do about it.

---

## The Nine Business Models of Commerce

Before we dive into the impact, let's map the territory. Every company that sells products fits into one of these nine models:

### **Brand-Owned Models** (You make the products)

1. **Direct-to-Consumer (D2C)** – You sell directly via your website/app (Warby Parker, Allbirds, Glossier)
2. **Hybrid Brand** – You sell both direct and through retailers (Nike, Lululemon, Patagonia)
3. **Marketplace-Hosted Brand** – You sell on platforms like Amazon/Etsy without your own storefront
4. **Subscription Brand** – Recurring revenue model (HelloFresh, Dollar Shave Club, Birchbox)
5. **Franchise/Distributor** – You own the IP, franchisees/distributors handle sales (automotive dealers, Snap-on Tools)

### **Retailer/Platform Models** (You sell others' products)

6. **Pure Retailer** – You aggregate brands but don't have your own products (Nordstrom, traditional department stores)
7. **Retailer with Private Label** – You sell both third-party and owned brands (Walmart, Target, Costco)
8. **Marketplace/Aggregator** – Multi-seller platform (Amazon, eBay, Etsy)
9. **B2B Wholesale** – You sell bulk/trade goods to businesses (Grainger, Sysco, industrial distributors)

Each model has different economics, customer relationships, and strategic advantages. And agentic commerce will impact them very differently.

## Model #1: Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) Brands

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Highly Positive** ✅✅✅

**Why D2C Brands Are Positioned to Win:**

D2C brands built their entire existence on direct customer relationships, first-party data, and modern digital infrastructure. Guess what? That's exactly what matters in the agentic era.

**The Structural Advantages:**

1. **You Own the Relationship:** When an AI agent shops for a customer, D2C brands can build their own agents or integrate directly with platforms. You're not depending on a retailer or marketplace to represent you.

2. **Data is Your Superpower:** You have comprehensive purchase history, preferences, behavioral data—everything agents need to make smart recommendations. Marketplace-hosted brands have none of this.

3. **You're Already API-First:** Most D2C brands built cloud-native, API-accessible infrastructure. Integrating with payment protocols (Google's AP2, OpenAI's ACP) is straightforward.

4. **Lower Customer Acquisition Costs:** Instead of paying Facebook/Google $50-150 per customer, agents discover your products based on actual needs. Your CAC could drop 40-70%.

**Real Example:**
Warby Parker spent millions on digital ads to acquire customers. In an agentic world, when someone tells their AI agent "I need prescription glasses that are stylish and affordable," the agent finds Warby Parker based on product attributes and reviews—not ad spend.

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate:** Implement all major payment protocols (AP2, ACP, TAP) within 90 days
- **Near-term:** Build semantic product catalogs with rich metadata agents can query
- **Long-term:** Consider launching your own brand agent to maintain customer relationships

**The Risk:**
Discovery challenges. Without retail presence or massive ad spend, you're dependent on agent algorithms to surface your brand. You need to optimize for "agent SEO"—making your products easy for AI to find and recommend.

**Bottom Line:** D2C brands should be excited. This is your opportunity to break free from the Google/Facebook duopoly and compete on product merit.

## Model #2: Hybrid Brands (D2C + Retail)

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Positive (But Complex)** ✅✅

**The Balancing Act:**

Hybrid brands like Nike (40% D2C, 60% retail) get the best of both worlds: direct customer relationships *and* retail distribution. But agentic commerce will force you to resolve a long-standing tension: **channel conflict**.

**What Will Happen:**

AI agents won't care about your channel strategy. When a customer asks for Nike running shoes, the agent will query both Nike.com and retail partners, instantly exposing any price differences. You can't hide channel-specific pricing anymore.

**The Opportunity:**

Smart hybrid brands will use agents for **intelligent routing**:
- High-value customers → Direct to your D2C channel (higher margin, own the data)
- Convenience-seekers → Nearest retail location with inventory (fast pickup)
- Price-sensitive → Best available deal across all channels

Nike could let agents choose: "Buy online for $120 with free shipping, or pick up today at Dick's Sporting Goods for $115."

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate:** Unify your commerce platform—single inventory view across D2C and retail
- **Resolve pricing:** Either standardize prices or clearly justify differences (exclusive features, services, etc.)
- **Partner with retailers:** Co-create agent strategies rather than competing for the same customer

**The Risk:**

If you can't resolve channel conflict, agents will exploit it. Retailers may resist if they see you favoring D2C through agent integrations. You need diplomatic strategy and technology that serves all channels.

**Bottom Line:** Hybrid brands have hedged distribution, which is smart. But you must get your house in order on pricing and data integration.

## Model #3: Marketplace-Hosted Brands

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Highly Negative** ⛔⛔⛔

**This is the model in the most danger.**

If you're a brand that primarily or exclusively sells through Amazon, eBay, Etsy, or similar marketplaces, agentic commerce represents an existential threat.

**Why This Model is Screwed:**

Let's count the layers of separation between you and the customer:

Consumer → AI Agent → Marketplace → Your Brand

That's **triple disintermediation**. You're three steps removed from the actual buyer.

**What You Lose:**

1. **No Customer Data:** The marketplace owns everything. You don't know who bought, their preferences, purchase history—nothing.
2. **No Direct Agent Integration:** You can't build APIs or integrate with payment protocols. The marketplace controls that.
3. **Pure Commoditization:** Agents will compare your product against dozens of competitors on the same marketplace, often purely on price.
4. **Marketplace Prioritization:** Amazon, Walmart, Target all have private labels. What do you think they'll tell their agents to recommend?

**The Nightmare Scenario:**

A customer asks ChatGPT to find the best wireless headphones under $100. ChatGPT queries Amazon. Amazon's algorithm (which controls what the agent sees) prioritizes Amazon Basics headphones over your brand. You never even get considered—despite being on the platform.

**Real Numbers:**

Amazon already takes 45% of seller revenue (up from 19% in 2014). Add agent integration costs, advertising to stay visible, and inevitable margin compression from agent price comparison, and you're looking at 60%+ of revenue going to Amazon.

**What You Need to Do:**

**Immediate (and I mean this week):**
1. **Launch a D2C channel.** This is not optional. This is existential.
2. Use Amazon Brand Registry to control unauthorized sellers
3. Start building direct customer relationships through packaging, email collection, anything you can

**Within 6 months:**
4. Shift marketing spend from Amazon advertising to D2C customer acquisition
5. Build first-party customer data
6. Evaluate whether marketplace sales are even viable long-term

**Within 18 months:**
7. Transition to D2C primary, marketplace secondary (or exit marketplace entirely)

**The Harsh Reality:**

For many marketplace-dependent brands, the answer is: **Get off the platform or die.**

You might need to abandon years of marketplace optimization. Yes, it's painful. But staying means permanent disintermediation with no customer data, no pricing power, and no differentiation.

**Bottom Line:** If you're reading this and your business is 70%+ marketplace sales, you have 12-24 months to diversify or you'll be commoditized out of existence.

## Model #4: Subscription Brands

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Extremely Positive** ✅✅✅✅

**This might be the single best business model for agentic commerce.**

Think about what AI agents are good at: repetitive, rule-based tasks with clear parameters. Now think about subscriptions: recurring purchases of the same or similar products on regular schedules.

**It's a perfect match.**

**Why Subscription Brands Will Thrive:**

1. **Agent-Managed Subscriptions:** Customer tells their agent, "Keep me stocked on healthy snacks, budget $50/month." The agent manages everything: initial selection, delivery timing, variety rotation, pause when on vacation, cancel when unneeded.

2. **Reduced Churn:** Currently, most subscriptions lose 50-67% of customers within 6 months. Why? Friction. Forgetting to pause, not adjusting delivery cadence, build-up of unused products. Agents eliminate all of this.

3. **Lower CAC:** Instead of spending $100-200 to acquire a subscriber through ads, agents discover subscriptions based on actual needs. HelloFresh doesn't need to retarget you 47 times—your agent finds them when you mention wanting easier meal planning.

4. **Dynamic Optimization:** Agents can adjust delivery timing based on consumption patterns. Out of coffee faster this month? Agent orders early. Skipped breakfast last week? Agent pushes delivery back.

**Real Example:**

Dollar Shave Club retains 40-50% of subscribers at 6 months—best in category. With agent management, that could jump to 60-70% because the agent removes all friction around pausing, modifying, or adjusting the subscription.

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate:** Build comprehensive subscription management APIs for agents (pause, skip, modify, cancel—all programmatic)
- **Near-term:** Create agent-managed subscription tiers with flexible parameters
- **Long-term:** Let agents fully control subscriptions—customer sets goals and budget, agent handles everything else

**The Risk:**

Switching becomes frictionless too. If your competitor offers better value, the agent can cancel and switch in seconds. You must continuously deliver value or agents will churn you ruthlessly.

**Bottom Line:** Subscription brands should sprint toward agent integration. This model is designed for it.

## Model #5: Franchise/Distributor Model

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Complex & Challenging** ⚠️⚠️

**The fundamental problem:** Your infrastructure is fragmented.

Franchises and distributor models spread operations across independent local operators. Each franchisee might have different systems, inventory, pricing, and capabilities. How do you present a unified interface to AI agents?

**The Challenge:**

An AI agent queries for "oil change near me under $50." Your franchise network has 200 locations, each with different:
- Inventory systems (can't see real-time availability)
- Pricing (some charge $45, some $55, some $65)
- Appointment scheduling systems (different booking platforms)
- Service capabilities (some locations offer more services than others)

How does the agent interact with this? It can't. It needs a unified API, single inventory view, consistent pricing, and centralized integration.

**The Opportunity (If You Invest):**

Corporate headquarters invests in unified commerce infrastructure:
1. Central API that agents can query
2. Real-time inventory across all franchise locations
3. Intelligent routing to nearest location with availability
4. Clear attribution rules (franchisee gets credit for agent-driven local sale)

Then you get:
- **Local Fulfillment:** Fast pickup/delivery through franchise network
- **Service Differentiation:** Local expertise, installation, support—things pure digital can't replicate
- **Geographic Coverage:** Physical presence in many markets

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate (Corporate must lead):** Invest in unified commerce platform for entire franchise network
- **Establish rules:** Clear attribution for agent-driven sales to specific franchisees
- **Get buy-in:** Involve franchisees early; they'll resist if imposed top-down
- **Connect inventory:** Real-time visibility across network
- **Near-term:** Launch agent routing to nearest franchisee with inventory
- **Long-term:** Franchisees become fulfillment/service layer; corporate owns agent discovery

**The Risk:**

If corporate doesn't invest, individual franchisees can't integrate on their own. You'll be invisible to agents while competitors with unified infrastructure win.

Alternatively, corporate might use agents to bypass franchisees entirely (selling direct), creating conflict.

**Bottom Line:** Franchise models face high implementation complexity but can succeed if corporate makes the infrastructure investment and aligns incentives properly.

## Model #6: Pure Retailer (No Private Label)

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Highly Negative** ⛔⛔⛔

**If you're a traditional retailer that only sells other brands' products (no private label), I have bad news: your business model may not survive.**

**The Problem:**

You're a pure intermediary. You don't make anything. You aggregate and resell other companies' products. In the old world, you added value through:
- Curation (selecting products to carry)
- Physical convenience (products in one place)
- Discovery (browsing and seeing what's available)
- Service (sales associates, expertise)

But in the agentic world:
- **Curation:** AI agents curate better than you (they query the entire internet)
- **Convenience:** Agents are more convenient than your store or website
- **Discovery:** Agents discover products better than browsing
- **Service:** For commodity products, agents don't need sales associates

**What's Left?**

For traditional retailers, the answer is uncomfortably close to "not much."

**The Disintermediation:**

Agents will go directly to brands when possible:
- Why buy Nike shoes from a department store when the agent can buy from Nike.com?
- Why pay the department store's markup when the agent can compare and get direct pricing?
- Why go through an intermediary when the brand has better product data and customer service?

**Real Example:**

Nordstrom sells premium brands. But what happens when a customer's agent can query all those brands directly, compare pricing across the internet, and execute purchases at the best price? Nordstrom becomes unnecessary.

Department stores are already struggling (80% reported sales declines vs. 22% for D2C brands). Agentic commerce accelerates this decline.

**What You Can Do:**

Honestly? Your options are limited:

1. **Launch Private Label Immediately** (see next section—this is your only real option)
2. **Become an "Agent Advisor"** (position as expert agents consult, but this is a weak position)
3. **Service Differentiation** (styling, alterations, expertise—but limited to high-touch categories)
4. **Exclusive Brand Partnerships** (negotiate exclusive products, but brands won't want this)
5. **Consider Exit** (merge, get acquired, or wind down)

**The Harsh Reality:**

Pure retail aggregation worked when consumers had high search/transaction costs. Agents reduce those costs to zero. Unless you differentiate, you're displaced.

**Bottom Line:** If you're a pure retailer without private label, you have 12-24 months to transform or you face obsolescence. This is not hyperbole.

## Model #7: Retailer with Private Label

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Positive to Very Positive** ✅✅✅

**This is the retail model that survives—and potentially thrives.**

Walmart (Great Value, Equate), Target (Good & Gather, Up & Up), and Costco (Kirkland) aren't just retailers. They're brands. And that changes everything.

**Why Private Label is Your Superpower:**

1. **Unique Products:** AI agents can't find your private label products anywhere else. You have differentiation.
2. **Pricing Control:** You control costs and margins, enabling competitive pricing without sacrificing profit.
3. **Customer Data:** You have comprehensive shopping data to inform product development and agent optimization.
4. **Higher Margins:** Private label margins are 20-30% higher than third-party products, protecting you from margin compression.

**The Numbers:**

- Private label: 24% of overall retail sales, growing faster than national brands (+2.3% vs. +1.1%)
- Walmart: 31% private label share (86% household penetration for Great Value)
- Target: 25% private label share, $30+ billion annual revenue
- Consumer perception: 80%+ rate private label quality same or better than national brands

**What Agents Do for You:**

When an agent compares "dishwasher detergent, best value, good quality," your private label can win because:
- Price advantage vs. national brands
- Quality data shows equivalence
- Exclusive availability drives purchase to your channel
- Customer reviews validate performance

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate:** Ensure private label has richer, more detailed data than national brands (agents need this to recommend)
- **Accelerate:** Increase private label to 40-50% of assortment over next 24 months
- **Agent-exclusive:** Create private label products or pricing available only through agent channels
- **Quality signaling:** Provide test results, comparison data, reviews that prove quality
- **Near-term:** Integrate with all major agent platforms
- **Long-term:** Make private label your primary differentiation; national brands become supplementary

**The Strategy:**

While national brands fight disintermediation, you leverage private label to become the destination agents choose. You're not just an intermediary—you're a brand.

**Real Example:**

Walmart's Bettergoods and Target's Dealworthy are the fastest-growing private labels in 2025 (200% volume growth each). These brands appeal to agents because they offer value without sacrificing quality.

**Bottom Line:** Retailers with strong private labels are well-positioned. Double down on this advantage aggressively.

## Model #8: Marketplace/Aggregator

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Complex & Uncertain** ⚠️⚠️⚠️

**Marketplaces like Amazon, eBay, and Etsy face a fascinating paradox:**

They're simultaneously **best positioned** and **most threatened** by agentic commerce.

**Why They Could Win:**

1. **Agent Integration Leaders:** Marketplaces were among the first to integrate with ChatGPT, Google agents (Etsy in ChatGPT, Walmart, Shopify)
2. **Comprehensive Inventory:** Agents can access millions of products through one API
3. **Trust Infrastructure:** Payment security, buyer protection, fraud detection already built
4. **Scale:** Volume makes marketplace integration attractive for agent platforms
5. **Data:** Comprehensive purchase behavior across categories and brands

**Why They Could Lose:**

1. **Disintermediation Risk:** Why pay marketplace 30-45% when agent can transact directly with sellers?
2. **Commoditization:** Agents reduce marketplace to price comparison engine
3. **Amazon Dominance:** If Amazon wins exclusive agent partnerships, other marketplaces become irrelevant
4. **Open Protocols:** If AP2/ACP enable direct agent-seller transactions, marketplace layer weakens

**The Existential Question:**

Do agents need marketplaces? Or can agents coordinate directly with brands/sellers?

**If marketplaces provide:**
- Trust (fraud protection, dispute resolution)
- Logistics (fulfillment, shipping)
- Aggregation (comprehensive inventory)

Then agents need them.

**But if open protocols** (Google AP2, OpenAI ACP) provide trust and payment infrastructure, and agents can query brands directly, marketplaces lose their reason to exist.

**What Marketplaces Must Do:**

- **Immediate:** Secure agent partnerships with ChatGPT, Google, Microsoft, Claude, others (this is a race)
- **Lower take rates:** May need to reduce to 20-30% to compete with direct transactions
- **Trust-as-a-service:** Emphasize fraud protection, buyer protection, dispute resolution
- **Private label acceleration:** Use data to create marketplace brands that perform well with agents
- **Strategic choice:** Open platform (embrace protocols) vs. walled garden (control integrations)

**The Likely Outcome:**

**Amazon wins** because of scale, data, logistics, and ability to secure exclusive/preferred agent partnerships.

Other marketplaces face existential risk if they can't secure comparable agent integrations. Etsy survives by owning artisan/handmade niche. eBay's future is uncertain.

**Bottom Line:** Marketplaces are in a race. Winners secure agent partnerships and provide trust infrastructure. Losers become irrelevant.

## Model #9: B2B Wholesale

### **Agentic Commerce Impact: Very Positive** ✅✅✅✅

**Surprise winner alert:** B2B wholesale might be the *most* positively impacted business model.

**Why B2B is Perfect for Agents:**

B2B procurement is:
- Repetitive (same products ordered regularly)
- Rule-based (procurement policies, spending limits, approval workflows)
- High-volume (many transactions per customer)
- Relationship-driven (ongoing account relationships)
- Data-rich (integrated with ERP, inventory systems)

These characteristics make B2B procurement **ideal for agent automation**.

**The Market Opportunity:**

- B2B e-commerce: $2.297 trillion (2024), growing 10.5% annually
- 80% of B2B transactions will be online by end of 2025 (Gartner)
- 73% of B2B buyers prefer online purchase
- 73% of B2B buyers are millennials (digital-native, expecting consumer-like experience)

**What Agents Do for B2B Wholesale:**

1. **Automated Replenishment:** Agent monitors customer inventory and reorders automatically when stock hits defined thresholds
2. **Intelligent Sourcing:** Agent compares suppliers on price, quality, delivery time, sustainability—executes orders with best option
3. **Spend Management:** Agent enforces procurement policies and spending limits automatically
4. **Contract Compliance:** Agent ensures purchases align with negotiated contract terms and pricing
5. **Predictive Procurement:** Agent forecasts needs based on production schedules, seasonality, historical patterns

**Real Example:**

Grainger (industrial supplies) customer uses agent to manage MRO (maintenance, repair, operations) supplies:
- Agent monitors which items are running low
- Compares Grainger pricing vs. competitors
- Automatically places orders within approved budget
- Routes urgent orders for next-day delivery, routine orders for standard shipping
- Generates spending reports for finance team

Result: Procurement team saves 20+ hours/week, spending stays within budget, stockouts reduced 90%.

**What You Need to Do:**

- **Immediate:** Build comprehensive APIs (catalog, pricing, ordering, inventory, shipping—all agent-accessible)
- **Create agent account types:** Special B2B account designed for agent management
- **Real-time everything:** Pricing, inventory, shipping estimates must be real-time for agent queries
- **Integration partners:** Partner with ERP (SAP, Oracle, NetSuite), inventory management, procurement platforms
- **Near-term:** Launch automated replenishment services
- **Long-term:** Full agent-managed procurement relationships with predictive ordering

**The Advantage:**

B2B relationships are complex. Agents augment rather than replace account managers. The human relationship remains for strategic decisions, while agents handle tactical execution.

**Bottom Line:** B2B wholesale should be sprinting toward agent integration. Efficiency gains are massive, and relationship complexity provides defensibility.

## What This Means for Your Business

### **If You're a Winner:**

Don't get complacent. Early-mover advantage matters. You have 6-12 months to establish agent integrations before competition catches up. Move fast.

**Action plan:**
- Integrate payment protocols (AP2, ACP, TAP) within 90 days
- Build agent-accessible APIs and semantic product catalogs
- Join ChatGPT, Google Shopping AI, other platforms
- Consider proprietary agent development
- Optimize for agent discovery and recommendation

### **If You're At Risk:**

You can win, but transformation is required. Half-measures won't work. Commit to infrastructure investment, strategic pivot, and new capabilities.

**Action plan:**
- Resolve structural issues (channel conflict, fragmented systems, pricing inconsistency)
- Invest in unified commerce platforms
- Accelerate differentiation (private label, exclusive products, unique value)
- Secure agent partnerships urgently
- Build agent integration capabilities rapidly

### **If You're Facing Existential Threat:**

Harsh reality check: Your current model may not be viable. You need to transform, diversify, or consider strategic alternatives.

**Action plan:**
- Launch D2C channel immediately (if marketplace-hosted brand)
- Add private label aggressively (if pure retailer)
- Reduce dependence on vulnerable channels within 12 months
- Build direct customer relationships and data
- Be prepared for difficult decisions (exit marketplace, close stores, merge, get acquired)

## The Bottom Line: Business Models Matter

Agentic commerce isn't a rising tide that lifts all boats. It's a reshuffling of the entire value chain.

**Value will accrue to:**
- Direct customer relationships
- Unique products (brands, private labels)
- Rich first-party data
- Modern, agent-accessible infrastructure
- Genuine differentiation beyond price

**Value will disappear from:**
- Pure intermediaries
- Fragmented, legacy systems
- Commodity aggregation
- Models dependent on information asymmetry
- Businesses without customer data or control

The transformation is already underway. Traffic from AI agents to retail sites increased 4,700% in the last year. ChatGPT has integrated Walmart, Shopify, Etsy, and PayPal. Google launched AP2 with 60+ partners. Visa and Mastercard launched agent payment protocols.

**The question isn't whether this will happen. It's happening.**

The question is: **Is your go-to-market model positioned to win or lose?**

You have 12-24 months to get on the right side of this transformation. The window is open—but closing fast.

Choose wisely. Move quickly. The future of commerce is being built right now.
